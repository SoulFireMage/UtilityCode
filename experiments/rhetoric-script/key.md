# The Ropewalk: companion key

*Read this awake, at a different time from the script. Its job is to give your conscious mind labels to hang on what the script does below the labels. Reading it right before the script would defeat the design, which is no named figures inside the trance.*

## The shape (the whole in one line)

One breath goes down through a working shed into a storm of voices, hits a single bare sentence, and comes back up the same way changed: every layer closes in a different style from the one it opened in.

## The architecture (step by step)

The script is a **ring composition** (also called chiastic structure): A – B – C – centre – C′ – B′ – A′. The whole text is built in the shape of the figure it teaches at sentence level, so a chiasmus contains chiasmi. For a long study of the form, see Mary Douglas, *Thinking in Circles* (2007).

| Layer | Opens as | Closes as |
|---|---|---|
| **A**: you, the room, breath | plain, second person, present tense | the same voice, now carrying echoes of everything between |
| **B**: the ropewalk | master speaks in periodic sentences, apprentice in fragments | **inverted**: apprentice speaks in periodic sentences, master in fragments |
| **C**: the auction barge | a catalogue of clashing registers, accelerating | ellipsis, counterfactual, cool aphorism |
| **Centre** | — | three plain words |

Three nested loops, not five, so the outer loops stay alive in working memory instead of going cold.

## Moves by section

**A, opening** ("Begin with breath…")
- *Correctio* (self-correction): "Not a deep one. Just the one you have."
- *Anaphora* with a *tricolon crescens* (three rising members): "You know… You have read… You have said…", each sentence longer than the last.
- Asyndetic tricolon (three items without "and"): "folded, stored, waiting."
- Word weight, monosyllable against polysyllable: *stone* / *inexhaustible*, "a fist and… an open hand."
- An unanswered rhetorical question, held open as the outermost loop.

**B, opening** (the ropewalk)
- Loose (cumulative) fragment: "Three hundred yards of it, low-roofed, lit from the side…"
- Antithesis plus ellipsis: "The ropemaker is old. The apprentice is not."
- **Periodic sentence** with suspension and resumptive repetition: "Before you twist anything… before you twist anything, you must know which way it wants to turn."
- Parataxis and asyndeton, with the subject dropped: "She nods. Picks up a yarn. Twists it. It kinks."
- *Anadiplosis* building to *gradatio* (climax): "Fibre makes yarn; yarn makes strand; strand makes rope…"
- *Sententia* (aphorism): "Anything that holds is something resisting something else."
- **Cumulative sentence** in Francis Christensen's sense, the base clause followed by stacked modifiers: "She walks the length of the shed with the yarn running out…"
- Onomatopoeia and isocolon (parallel clauses of matching length): "The wheel whirrs. The top creaks."
- *Hypophora* (ask, then answer) sliding into free indirect style: "Was he mocking her…? He was not… its own kind of unbearable."
- *Polyptoton* (one root in several forms): "You twist; it is twisted; it remembers being twisted."
- Simile corrected into metaphor: "is like a dead eel… No: *is* a dead eel."
- *Litotes* (understatement by negation): "No small thing."
- *Antimetabole*: "The twist keeps the strand, and the strand keeps the twist."
- *Aposiopesis* (breaking off) as the hand-off to C: "gone by—"

**C, opening** (the auction)
- The teller dissolves into his tale: "Listen," he says, then quotation marks drop away.
- **Lot One**, purple: an exclamatory **loose (cumulative)** sentence, its main clause ("Consider… this ceremonial mantle") first and the modifiers trailing, with polysyndeton, synaesthesia ("drinks the lantern-light and exhales"), tricolon ("so liquid, so indolently cool, so shamelessly whispered").
- **Lot Two**, forensic: nominal style, impersonal or passive voice, numerals, colon-led catalogue. A surreal object reported in deadpan.
- **Lot Three**, wit: *zeugma / syllepsis*, one verb yoking two senses ("left in haste and a hired carriage", "took her diamonds and her leave", "broke the seal, her promise, and… the bank"); bathos ("four reputations and one remarkably good tablecloth"); comic simile; mock-rhetorical question.
- **Lot Four**, hardboiled: fragments, asyndeton, second-person imperatives, anaphora ("watch… watch… watch"), isocolon ("Rust in the grooves. Salt in the pits."), closing antithesis.
- **Lot Five**, covert blank verse: strict iambic pentameter throughout, set as prose ("her painted eyes half-eaten by the brine"). Elegiac register.
- **Lot Six**, telegraphese: articles dropped, the "STOP" rhythm, a closing paradox ("ARRIVING YESTERDAY").
- **Lot Seven**, litany: liturgical anaphora ("For the…"), anadiplosis ("the drowned, and the drowned"), chiasmus ("the tongue that forgets what it swore, and the oath that outlives the tongue"), the optative "Give us", and *aposiopesis* as the squall cuts off the prayer ("will not break—").
- **The storm**: alliterative compounds in sprung rhythm, Hopkins-like ("black-backed, brine-blind, wind-whipped"); participle chains; *accumulatio*; polysyndeton ("and a magistrate… and a thief"); *epistrophe* ("screaming… screaming… screaming"); crescendo broken off mid-sentence.

**Centre**
- "The rope held." Plainness as a move: maximum weight carried by minimum means, landing right after maximum ornament.

**C, closing**
- Fragments; *epizeuxis* (immediate repetition) with a pun: "slower, slower, still."
- Diminution: after the great catalogue, a catalogue of small things ("a dropped glove. Three copper coins.").
- Anaphoric negation turning to correction: "Not the storm, not the shouting, not the gold; only the hush…"
- Counterfactual subjunctive: "Had you been standing…", "You might have…"
- Isocolon: "too heavy for copper, too quiet for gold."
- *Mise en abyme* (the story containing an image of itself): the ledger numbered in reverse, warm "as though someone had just finished reading it aloud."
- The teller's "I" re-emerges ("my hawser") as the loop closes.
- Parenthesis: "—never by strain—". *Anastrophe* (inverted word order): "Strange cargo they carried."

**B, closing**
- Speaker inversion: the apprentice now owns the periodic sentence. This is B-open mirrored, a chiasmus at scene level.
- *Stichomythia* (rapid one-line exchanges): "Thick enough." / "For a gale?" …
- Zeugma: "Brushes the hemp dust from his knees and the story from his face."
- Register bleed: Lot Two's forensic inventory leaks into the shed ("Wheel: one, oak, sound.").
- Antimetabole: "Left against right, right against left; tension making stillness, stillness holding tension."
- Covert iambic pentameter: "the tide comes in the way it always has."

**A, closing**
- Paradox: "The same breath… and not the same."
- Paired antitheses recalling the registers: "The velvet and the tin, the wit and the iron…"
- **Mimetic syntax**: sentences that perform what they describe ("Short, when it should be short." / "Long, when the thought needs room to turn…").
- A *tetracolon* of similes (four parallel members); the opening question answered.
- **Exit ramp**: three generation prompts (short, periodic, strange) that hand the voice to your own page.

## Why it's built this way

Each claim is tagged with its standing.

- **Enactment over labels**: saying a figure aloud trains the ear and mouth; naming it mid-flow pulls attention into critic mode. *Plausible, design judgement.*
- **Contrast**: a dimension becomes noticeable when it varies while other things hold still (contrasting cases; variation theory). *Established for noticing form.*
- **Structural priming**: producing a structure makes producing it again more likely. *Established, though often short-lived, which suits a warm-up.*
- **Story content mirrors the target**: rope as sentence, tension and release, an apprentice whose hands learn before her head. *Established as practice (Ericksonian metaphor); the mechanism is speculation.*
- **Generation at the end only**: blanks mid-script would break the rhythm and fail if the script is read to you. *Design judgement.*

## Not yet covered (candidates for a second script)
