# White Falcon: model notes

`white_falcon_model.blend` (Blender 5.2.1) is a hard-surface model of Gawain's starship, built to the
`MODELLING_BRIEF.md` spec. It measures **25.5 m wide × 25.07 m long × 4.42 m tall**, with **17,080 triangles**
after mirroring and **0 zero-area faces**.

## Silhouette match (Blender Cycles ortho renders vs traced Meshy references)

| View  | IoU   |
|-------|-------|
| Top   | 0.948 |
| Right | 0.886 |
| Back  | 0.861 |

The target was 0.85 for each view. `model_preview.jpg` shows each reference with the model silhouette
overlaid in cyan, plus a front 3/4 and a rear beauty render. The raw renders and red/green overlays are in `renders/`
(red = reference only, green = model only).

## How it's built

Everything is generated by a script, so it can be rebuilt and retuned:

```
python model_gen.py                      # parts -> model_parts.json (numpy + PIL)
python silhouette_check.py parts         # quick IoU from projected faces, no Blender needed
blender -b -P build_model.py             # build .blend, GPU (OptiX) renders, save, export .glb
python silhouette_check.py renders renders
python make_preview.py                   # model_preview.jpg
```

- **Blades, hull, spikes, crest, fin and keel** are quad lofts along a spine curve. Each part has a short list of
  spine control points plus profiles along its length: height, top and bottom thickness, and *cant* (how much the
  section tilts outward). The plan-view width at each station is **sampled from the traced top silhouette**, so the
  top outline follows the Meshy reference. The heights were tuned against the side and back silhouettes.
- The cross-section is faceted for a hard-surface read: a sharp outer edge, a **navy chamfer band**, the pearl
  top panel and a centre ridge, with a navy underside. Facet edges are marked sharp, and a Weighted Normal
  modifier keeps the shading crisp.
- **Cyan slots** are small emissive quads sitting just proud of the navy chamfer bands, in the same mesh as the
  blade, so they move with it.
- **Pods** and **engines** are lathed (revolved) profiles. Each engine has a navy nacelle, a pearl lip, a cyan ring,
  a navy recess, an inner cyan ring and a navy cone. **Conduits** are tubes along curved paths.
- Every part has its own object in the `Ship` collection. Each one has a live **Mirror (X)** modifier that mirrors
  about the `Mirror_Centre` empty at the world origin. Centreline parts have clipping and merge on, so their seam stays welded.
  The origins sit at each part's centre (on X = 0 for centreline parts), so you can grab and tweak one part at a time.
- Materials: `Pearl_Hull` (light pearl, clear coat), `Navy_Recess`, and `Cyan_Glow` (emission 5).
- The `REF_Top`, `REF_Right` and `REF_Back` image empties are kept from the blockout and packed into the file.
  `Stage` holds the camera and three area lights used for the beauty render.

## What's a guess

- **The references disagree in places.** In the back view the spike tips sit at ±13.6 m, but the top view (and the
  brief's 25.5 m width) puts them at ±12.75 m. I followed the top view, which costs a few points of back-view IoU.
  The back view's crest peak (2.5 m) is also higher than the side view's (2.2 m), so the model splits the difference.
- **The rear body is chunky** (about 3 m deep at the shoulders) because both the side and back silhouettes demand it.
  How that depth breaks up into panels is my interpretation.
- **The engines' depth** (how far the nacelles run forward) and the conduit paths are invented, since they're hidden
  in every reference. Their placement is from the rear redraw, fitted inside the back silhouette.
- **The pods sit almost flush** because the back silhouette leaves no room above about 1.15 m at x = ±3.4 m.
- **Surface detail is minimal.** The Meshy model's ribbing, the slot inside each side blade and the gaps in the back
  view's lower body are not modelled.

## Suggestions for refining

1. **The rear hull and crest are the lumpiest areas.** Core, crest and shoulders overlap, and from behind they read
   as blobs. A hand-modelled rear deck that merges them would be the biggest visual improvement.
2. **Panel the pearl tops**: inset or knife a few panel lines with navy recesses, as in the concept art.
3. **Add a Bevel modifier** (2 segments, angle-limited, about 2 cm) above the Weighted Normal one to catch highlights on the sharp edges.
4. **Rib the gap between each side blade and the hull** (the ribbed, glowing area in the concept art) as a separate part.
5. **Tune heights and widths in `model_gen.py`** if you'd rather adjust numbers than vertices. Each part's
   `z` / `ht` / `hb` / `k` / `kt` / `cap` tables are keyed by position along the spine (0 = front tip). After a
   rebuild, check the IoU again.
